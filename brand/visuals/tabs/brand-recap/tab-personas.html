Four personas · priority order

One audience archetype: builders who ship. Four sub-personas ordered by priority. When in doubt, write for Marco. He wants output, not philosophy — things that run without him, specific proof points, the promise that he is not the bottleneck.

The OSS thesis leads with Nadia (sovereignty as a practical survival tool), not with Priya (ideology). Philosophy belongs on the Constitution page (`/constitution/`). CTAs belong to Marco.

§3 · Persona cards — P1 → P4
P1 · primary Marco — The Multiplier

Solo founder, 1-person internal tools team, freelancer punching above weight. Doesn't want to manage people — wants to manage systems. His ceiling is hours, not skill, and he knows it.

Trigger Turns down a project for lack of capacity, then immediately looks for ways to add capacity without hiring. JTBD Deploy a reliable agentic foundation that handles repeatable tasks without constant supervision — so he can focus on work that requires judgment.
"Every time I build something that runs without me, I get a day back. I want a hundred of those things running."
P2 · secondary Nadia — The Autonomy Seeker

Burned by SaaS shutdown. Vendor pricing survivor. Open source as survival strategy, not ideology. Checks the license before reading the README. Checks the last commit date and the business model before starring.

Trigger Pricing email from a tool central to her workflow; she immediately searches for alternatives. JTBD Run agentic and automation infrastructure she fully controls — no SaaS dependency, no vendor lock, portable to any machine she owns.
"Every time I depend on something I can't run myself, I'm borrowing from my own future."
P3 · tertiary Jordan — The Assembler

Technical product person, solo founder who learned to code, systems thinker. Upstream contributor when the gap hurts. Writes the bridge code, then owns it forever.

Trigger Spends a full day on glue code that should have existed and decides it's the last time. JTBD Connect disparate tools into a coherent system without writing more infrastructure glue than necessary — and without owning every layer.
"I don't mind complexity — complexity is just what happens when real things interact. What I mind is unnecessary complexity. I just want the pieces to fit."
P4 · quaternary Priya — The Commons Builder

OSS maintainer, blogs about what she builds, gives back without expectation. Believes in the commons. Builds in public because the stack should belong to everyone.

Trigger Discovers a Roxabi project that does almost exactly what she was planning to build herself — and it's already open and forkable. JTBD Build on a foundation that will still be maintained in two years — and that her contributions can extend, not just use.
"I contribute because someone before me contributed and that's why I had something to build on. It's not complicated."
§3 · Primary audience signal
When in doubt, write for Marco. He responds to: things that run without him, specific proof points, and the promise that he is not the bottleneck. He will not wait through a manifesto to get to the product. He needs to see the foundation before he reads about the philosophy.
